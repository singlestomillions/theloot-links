Short-form video is the cheapest way for a small creator to get discovered right now — but filming, editing, captioning, and posting every day burns hours. AI video tools can cut that workload down. The honest catch: every "free" plan in this category is a trial in disguise. Free tiers are for testing whether a tool fits your workflow, not for running a channel forever. Here's how the main options stack up, based on real use.

1. Fliki — best for faceless videos from a script

Try Fliki free

Fliki turns a written script into a finished video: it pairs your text with stock footage or AI visuals, adds an AI voiceover, and burns in captions. It's the fastest route to a "faceless" channel format — commentary, listicles, facts, story narrations — where you never appear on camera.

Pros - Script-to-video in minutes; you write, it assembles - Large library of AI voices in many languages and accents - Captions and aspect-ratio exports (9:16, 1:1, 16:9) built in - Genuinely usable free tier for testing the workflow before paying anything

Cons - The free plan is limited — a small monthly credit allowance that runs out fast if you post daily, and free exports carry restrictions - AI voices still sound synthetic on emotional or fast-paced scripts; the best voices sit behind paid plans - Stock footage matching is hit-or-miss — expect to swap some clips manually

Best for: creators who can write but don't want to film or record their own voice.

2. OpusClip — best for turning long videos into shorts

Try OpusClip free

OpusClip does the opposite job: you feed it a long video — a podcast, livestream, webinar, or long YouTube video — and it finds the moments most likely to work as standalone clips, cuts them, captions them, and reframes them vertically.

Pros - Real time-saver if you already produce anything long-form; one recording becomes many posts - Auto-captions and vertical reframing that tracks the speaker - Scores clips so you can pick which ones to post first - Free tier gives you a monthly credit allowance to test it on your own footage

Cons - Useless if you have no long-form source material — it clips, it doesn't create - Its picks are algorithmic, not editorial; it sometimes chooses loud moments over meaningful ones, so review before posting - Credits are consumed by processing time, so long source videos eat the free allowance quickly

Best for: podcasters, streamers, coaches, and anyone sitting on long recordings they never repurposed.

3. Metricool — the unglamorous tool that keeps you consistent

Try Metricool free

Metricool isn't a video generator — it's the scheduling and analytics layer. You plan posts for TikTok, Instagram, YouTube, and more from one calendar, and it shows you which posts actually performed, and when your audience is online.

Pros - One calendar for multiple accounts and platforms instead of posting from each app - Analytics that show best posting times and per-post performance in plain language - Free plan exists and is enough for a single creator to start scheduling

Cons - It doesn't make content — it only distributes and measures it, so it can't rescue a weak video - Free plan limits (accounts, scheduled posts, history depth) push active creators toward paid tiers - Some platform features (certain post types, first-comment tricks) still work better natively in each app

Best for: anyone whose real problem isn't ideas — it's posting consistently and knowing what worked.

4. Flixier — best for editing in your browser

Try Flixier free

Flixier is the plain editor of the group: a full video editor that runs entirely in your browser. You upload clips, cut them on a timeline, add text, transitions, and music, and it renders in the cloud — so a weak laptop or a Chromebook can still turn around a finished short without installing anything.

Pros - Nothing to install — it runs in the browser and renders fast because the heavy work happens in the cloud - A real timeline editor, so you keep manual control instead of trusting an algorithm's picks - Handy extras for creators: screen recording, templates, and easy resizing for different platforms

Cons - It edits — it doesn't create footage or write your script, so you still need something to edit - Being browser-based means it leans on your internet connection; big uploads on slow Wi-Fi are painful - The free plan is a test tier with limits and watermarked exports, not a permanent editing setup

Best for: creators who already have footage and just want a fast, simple editor that works on any computer.

How to choose (the honest version)

  • You have scripts but no camera presence: start with Fliki.
  • You have long videos gathering dust: start with OpusClip.
  • You have content but no consistency: start with Metricool.
  • You have footage and just need to edit it anywhere: start with Flixier.

And one rule that will save you money: exhaust the free tier before paying for anything. Post for two weeks on free plans. If a tool's limits are genuinely what's stopping you — not your posting habit — that's when an upgrade earns its price. If you're not posting, a paid plan just makes not posting more expensive.
